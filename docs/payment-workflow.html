AffiliatePoppy · design note · revised 16 August 2026 (D18 — collect at source)

How the money moves

Every payment path, every date, and exactly what the ledger shows — including the ones where something goes wrong. Revised for D18: the commission is collected at the moment of sale, inside the platform fee — nobody owes anybody afterwards.

Read this first: there are two separate products here

Almost all the complexity lives in the second one, which is optional. Keeping them apart is the difference between a simple story and a confusing one.

The sentence for an investor: the product we sell today counts money and never touches it. The platform-level programme collects the publisher's commission inside the platform fee, at the instant of each sale — so AgentsPoppy pays publishers out of money it already holds, and there is no debt, no chasing, and no credit risk anywhere in the system.
World B · the unit

One sale, and where every euro goes

A €100 poppy, 5% customer discount, 10% publisher commission. The buyer enters the code in the poppy, before checkout — that one detail is what makes everything below possible: because the platform creates the checkout knowing the code, it applies the discount and sets its fee to platform 5% + the publisher's 10% in the same instant. (A code typed later, on Stripe's own page, would be too late — the fee is immutable once the checkout exists.)

Why AgentsPoppy pays the publisher

So the publisher has one counterparty who can never answer "talk to the developer". That promise is the product — and under D18 it is backed by money already in hand, not by a claim on someone else.

Why renewals need nothing new

A subscription started with a code carries the bumped fee percentage on every renewal — Stripe collects platform share and commission automatically, month after month, with no further machinery.

World B · timing

The calendar — a date, not a delay

Publishers do not need to be paid quickly; they need to know exactly when. The schedule is one sentence: sales in a month are paid on the 1st of the month after next. (It was the 10th when the platform still had to debit developers first and wait for the debits to settle — D18 removed that step, so the payout moved nine days earlier.)

What the wait is for — refunds, and only refunds

The money is already collected, so the wait protects exactly one thing: the window in which a sale can come undone before the publisher has been paid. A refund inside the clearing period costs nobody anything — the entry cancels, and the commission share of the fee goes back to the developer's account the same day.

Both worlds · the ledger

The life of a ledger entry

Every sale becomes one row, carrying its state and the date it will pay from the moment it appears. With collection gone, the life is simpler: Pending until the cohort clears, then Paid — with two exits for refunds.

The report-and-collect path is the fallback, not the norm. For AgentsPoppy every poppy sale goes through the platform's own checkout, so this path handles edge cases only. For merchants whose sub-sellers run their own checkouts, it is the product — AffiliatePoppy's "Owed back to you" ledger card exists exactly for them.

6 Someone tries the obvious fraud

A developer signs up as a publisher, "sells" their own poppy to themselves with their own code, waits for the commission, then refunds. The arithmetic, wearing all three hats:

StepTheir position
Pays, as the fake customer−€95.00
Receives, as the merchant (net of the €14.25 fee)+€80.75
Receives, as the publisher (on payout day)+€9.50
Refund: €95 back as customer, €80.75 returned as merchant, €9.50 fee share returned to them as developernet +€9.50 − €9.50 = ±€0.00
Publisher-side reversal — a deduction against future earnings, not a payment out±€0.00

The platform's €4.75, returned on refund? No — the platform share follows its refund policy−€4.75
Net result of the fraud≈ −€6.45 per attempt
Still unprofitable — by arithmetic, not policy. The scheme only pays if a reversal can pull cash out of the platform, and it never can: the publisher side is a deduction, the developer side is the return of money that was theirs anyway. Collusion doesn't help — one of the colluders has to be the merchant eating the fees. On top: a developer cannot be a publisher on their own poppy (both identities are known at minting), and a refund pattern stops minting on that account.

The five rules everything above follows from

  1. The party who issued the code pays the publisher. One counterparty, who can never say "talk to the developer".
  2. The commission is collected at the moment of sale, inside the platform fee — the buyer enters the code in the poppy, before checkout, so the fee can carry it. Nobody owes, nobody chases, nobody defaults.
  3. The platform always pays out of money it already holds. Collection precedes payout by construction, not by scheduling.
  4. A fixed calendar date beats a short one. Sales in a month pay on the 1st of the month after next, and every ledger row shows its date from birth.
  5. A reversal is a deduction or a return, never a payment out. This is what makes fraud arithmetically unprofitable rather than merely forbidden.
And the boundary, once more. This document describes central campaigns — the platform-level programme. AffiliatePoppy as it ships does none of the money movement: it mints the codes on every participating account, counts every sale wherever it lands (both halves live-verified on 16 August 2026), and reports what is owed. The merchant pays publishers from their own bank; the D18 fee mechanics live in the platform's checkout, not in the poppy.
